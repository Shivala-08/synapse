# Neurotransmitters in ADHD

## Dopamine

### Role in ADHD
Dopamine is the primary neurotransmitter implicated in ADHD. It plays crucial roles in:
- **Reward processing**: Motivation and reinforcement learning
- **Executive function**: Working memory, planning, and impulse control
- **Motor control**: Movement regulation and coordination
- **Attention**: Sustained focus and task engagement

### Dopamine Pathways
1. **Mesolimbic pathway**: Reward and motivation (VTA → Nucleus Accumbens)
2. **Mesocortical pathway**: Executive function (VTA → Prefrontal Cortex)
3. **Nigrostriatal pathway**: Motor control (Substantia Nigra → Striatum)
4. **Tuberoinfundibular pathway**: Prolactin regulation

### Dopamine Receptors
- **D1-like family** (D1, D5): Excitatory, enhance signal processing
- **D2-like family** (D2, D3, D4): Inhibitory, modulate neural activity
- D4 receptor variants associated with ADHD susceptibility

## Norepinephrine

### Role in ADHD
Norepinephrine regulates:
- **Arousal**: Wakefulness and alertness
- **Attention**: Selective and sustained attention
- **Impulse control**: Behavioral inhibition
- **Stress response**: Fight-or-flight reactions

### Norepinephrine Pathways
- **Locus coeruleus → Cortex**: Attention and arousal
- **Locus coeruleus → Limbic system**: Emotional regulation

## Serotonin

### Role in ADHD
- Regulates mood, sleep, and appetite
- Interacts with dopamine systems
- Implicated in emotional dysregulation in ADHD
- Serotonin transporter gene (5-HTT) variants associated with ADHD

## GABA (Gamma-Aminobutyric Acid)

### Role in ADHD
- Primary inhibitory neurotransmitter
- Regulates neural excitability
- GABA deficits may contribute to hyperactivity
- Interacts with dopamine and norepinephrine systems

## Glutamate

### Role in ADHD
- Primary excitatory neurotransmitter
- Involved in learning and memory
- Glutamate-dopamine interactions affect executive function
- May contribute to cognitive symptoms in ADHD

## Key Takeaways
- Dopamine dysregulation is central to ADHD pathophysiology
- Norepinephrine contributes to attention and impulse control symptoms
- Multiple neurotransmitter systems interact in ADHD
- Medications target these systems (stimulants increase dopamine/norepinephrine)
