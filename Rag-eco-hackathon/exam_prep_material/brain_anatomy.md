# Brain Anatomy and ADHD

## Prefrontal Cortex

### Structure and Function
- **Location**: Frontal lobe, anterior to motor cortex
- **Subregions**:
  - **Dorsolateral PFC**: Working memory, planning
  - **Ventromedial PFC**: Decision-making, emotion
  - **Orbitofrontal cortex**: Impulse control, social behavior
  - **Anterior cingulate**: Error monitoring, conflict resolution

### ADHD Relevance
- Reduced volume and activity in ADHD
- Thinner cortical thickness
- Delayed maturation (2-3 years behind typically developing peers)
- Impaired executive function network

## Basal Ganglia

### Structure
- **Components**: Caudate nucleus, putamen, globus pallidus, subthalamic nucleus
- **Function**: Motor control, habit formation, reward processing

### ADHD Relevance
- Smaller caudate nucleus volume
- Reduced activation during inhibitory control
- Impaired reward processing pathways
- Connected to dopamine system dysfunction

## Cerebellum

### Structure and Function
- **Location**: Posterior brain, beneath occipital lobes
- **Functions**: Motor coordination, timing, working memory
- **Vermis**: Posture, gait, speech timing

### ADHD Relevance
- Reduced cerebellar volume, especially vermis
- Impaired timing and synchronization
- Affects motor coordination and cognitive timing
- Connected to prefrontal cortex via circuits

## Corpus Callosum

### Structure
- **Function**: Connects left and right hemispheres
- **Components**: Genu (front), body, splenium (back)

### ADHD Relevance
- Reduced thickness in ADHD
- Impaired interhemispheric communication
- Affects processing speed
- May contribute to attention difficulties

## Default Mode Network (DMN)

### Components
- **Medial PFC**: Self-referential thought
- **Posterior cingulate**: Memory retrieval
- **Precuneus**: Self-awareness
- **Angular gyrus**: Semantic processing

### ADHD Relevance
- **DMN interference**: DMN fails to deactivate during tasks
- **Reduced anticorrelation**: DMN and task-positive network don't suppress each other
- **Mind wandering**: Persistent DMN activation during attention tasks
- **Network dynamics**: Impaired switching between DMN and task networks

## Key Takeaways
- ADHD involves multiple brain regions and networks
- Prefrontal cortex dysfunction is central to executive function deficits
- Basal ganglia abnormalities affect reward and motor control
- Default Mode Network interference contributes to inattention
- Brain development in ADHD shows delayed maturation patterns
